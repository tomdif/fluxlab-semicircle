01 / GEOMETRY

Specify what “half a loop” means.

Take a radius a > 0 and a steady current I flowing counterclockwise along the upper semicircle in the xy-plane. The arc starts at (+a, 0, 0) and ends at (−a, 0, 0). Its parameterization and prefactor are

r′(φ) = a(cosφ, sinφ, 0),   0 ≤ φ ≤ π;   C = μ₀I4π.

An isolated open arc cannot sustain a steady current without other parts of a circuit. We first calculate the arc’s contribution. A closed semicircular loop includes a specified return path; here that is the diameter, with current flowing from −a to +a.

The formulas use coordinates relative to the coil center and its local XY plane. The explorer rotates and translates each coil before combining its contribution. All equations below use SI units; the explorer displays field in microtesla.

02 / EXACT INTEGRAL

Start with all three components.

Biot–Savart gives B(r) = C ∫ dl′ × (r − r′) / |r − r′|³. Define the source-to-observer distance

D(φ)² = a² + x² + y² + z² − 2a(x cosφ + y sinφ).

Taking the cross product yields the full field:

Barc(x,y,z) = Ca ∫0πz cosφ ex + z sinφ ey + (a − x cosφ − y sinφ)ezD(φ)³dφ.

This single integral already specifies the field everywhere off the arc. There is no rotational symmetry about z, so its transverse components generally do not vanish. The next steps reduce it to standard special functions.

03 / CLOSED FORM

Reduce the field to two elliptic integrals.

Set x = ρ cosθ, y = ρ sinθ, with θ = atan2(y,x), and define

S = a² + ρ² + z²,   d = √((a + ρ)² + z²),   q = (a − ρ)² + z²,
m = 4aρ / d²,   t₀ = −(π + θ)/2,   t₁ = −θ/2.

We use the parameter convention m = k², not the modulus convention. Define the real-amplitude incomplete elliptic integrals

F(t | m) = ∫0t du√(1 − m sin²u),
E(t | m) = ∫0t √(1 − m sin²u) du.

Continue these integrals along the real amplitude axis. Reducing the two endpoint amplitudes modulo π independently would lose complete-integral contributions.

H(t | m) = E(t | m) − m sin t cos t√(1 − m sin²t).

For ρ > 0 and q > 0, introduce

J₁ = 2d[F(t₁ | m) − F(t₀ | m)],
J₃ = 2dq[H(t₁ | m) − H(t₀ | m)].

The endpoint distances are R₊ = √((x − a)² + y² + z²) and R₋ = √((x + a)² + y² + z²). The answer in the cylindrical basis is

Bρ = Cz2ρ(SJ₃ − J₁),
Bθ = Czρ(1/R₊ − 1/R₋),
Bz = C2[J₁ + (a² − ρ² − z²)J₃].

Convert back using Bx = Bρ cosθ − Bθ sinθ and By = Bρ sinθ + Bθ cosθ.

Why the reduction works

With u = φ − θ, D² = S − 2aρ cos u. The two scalar quantities are J₁ = ∫₀^π D⁻¹ dφ and J₃ = ∫₀^π D⁻³ dφ. Consequently,

∫0π cos uD³dφ = SJ₃ − J₁2aρ,
∫0π sin uD³dφ = 1/R₊ − 1/R₋aρ.

The first follows by writing 2aρ cos u = S − D². The second follows from differentiating 1/D with respect to φ. Finally, t = (u − π)/2 gives D² = d²(1 − m sin²t), while

H′(t | m) = 1 − m(1 − m sin²t)3/2,   1 − m = q/d².

Substitution establishes (2) and then (3). No elliptic integral of the third kind is needed.

04 / EXCEPTIONAL COORDINATES

Cover the points where the formula needs a limit.

On the perpendicular centerline, ρ = 0: direct integration of (1) is elementary.

Barc(0,0,z) = C(a² + z²)3/2(2az ey + πa² ez).

The sideways component is nonzero when z ≠ 0. At the center itself, Barc(0) = μ₀I/(4a) ez.

On the unoccupied lower half of the circle: q = 0 is only an apparent singularity of the elliptic representation. For ρ = a, z = 0, −π < θ < 0,

Barc = C2a ln[tan((π − θ)/4)tan(−θ/4)] ez.

In contrast, the upper semicircle and its endpoints are actual current-carrying filament locations. The ideal-wire field there is not assigned a finite value. The explorer excludes the entered conductor radius; it does not calculate the interior field of a finite wire.

05 / COMPLETE CIRCUIT

Add the straight return.

For a diameter return flowing from (−a, 0, 0) to (+a, 0, 0), write s² = y² + z². The exact segment contribution, for s > 0, is

Bdiameter = C −z ey + y ezs²[x + aR₋ − x − aR₊].
Bloop = Barc + Bdiameter.

On the line y = z = 0 outside the diameter segment, the segment contribution is zero. On the segment itself, the filament field is undefined. In particular, the origin is on the wire of the diameter-closed loop.

For z ≠ 0 on the perpendicular centerline, the total becomes

Bloop(0,0,z) = C(a² + z²)3/2[−2a³zey + πa²ez].
Try it: a = 5 cm, I = 10 A, P = (0, 0, 5 cm).

The arc gives (0, +14.142, 22.214) µT. The diameter adds (0, −28.284, 0) µT. The closed loop therefore gives (0, −14.142, 22.214) µT.

06 / PHYSICAL CHECKS

Test the structure of the answer.

  • Current reversal: reversing I reverses every component of B.
  • In the plane: for z = 0 away from the wire, the field is perpendicular to that plane.
  • Full circular loop: adding the complementary semicircle cancels the transverse centerline field and gives Bz = μ₀Ia²/[2(a² + z²)3/2].
  • Large distance: the closed loop has magnetic moment m = Iπa²/2 ez, so its leading field is the dipole field below. The arc alone has a 1/r² endpoint contribution, canceled by the return.
Bloop(r) = μ₀4πr³[3(m · r̂)r̂ − m] + O(μ₀Ia³/r⁴).
07 / INTERPRETATION

Exact model, numerical evaluations.

The derivation is an exact identity in the free-space filament model. The field map uses adaptive Gauss–Legendre quadrature. The comparison evaluates the elliptic representation using adaptive Simpson integration for F and E; special centerline and lower-circle cases use the elementary expressions above. The two numerical routes may lose accuracy near ill-conditioned coordinates, where the comparison reports that limitation rather than claiming agreement.

The separate current optimizer is an application of superposition. Its RMS is target mismatch, not numerical solver error. A nonzero efficiency preference can trade a higher RMS for lower heating. Enforcing a tighter current limit can also worsen the fit relative to an initially infeasible current setting. Sampling a region does not certify the field between sample points.

Finite winding packs, magnetic materials, temperature rise, AC effects, plasma response, and mechanical forces require additional models. These equations specify the field of the stated sources, not a new propulsion mechanism or a proof of plasma confinement.
